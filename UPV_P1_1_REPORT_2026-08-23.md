# UPV assessment, P1-1 matched-normal validation, and literature review

Date: 2026-08-23  
Scope: P4 and P6 cSCC (GRCh37), DCIS1 and DCIS2 (GRCh38), current
`baseQ0mapQ0` SPARCAL trees. No production VCF was rewritten.

## Executive findings

1. **P1-1 validates the population-panel route, not general germline discovery.** At
   Visium RNA depth >=10, exact-allele sensitivity against matched-normal WES was 0.718
   in P4 and 0.666 in P6 overall. It was 0.844 and 0.796 for exact 1KGP alleles, but
   only 0.059 and 0.016 for WES truth alleles absent from 1KGP. The defensible positive
   result is therefore recovery of expressed, panel-resolved germline variants.
2. **Raw genotype agreement is good at adequate RNA depth.** Among exact SPARCAL
   detections at RNA depth >=10, upstream raw GT agreed with normal-DNA GT for 91.6% of
   P4 sites and 87.5% of P6 sites. Homozygous-alternate agreement was 98.9--99.5%; the
   lower heterozygous agreement (79.8--87.3% for panel alleles) is consistent with
   allelic dropout/imbalance in RNA. SparcalNet had only 27 P4 and 11 P6 evaluable model
   decisions at this threshold and does not support a genotype-correction claim.
3. **UPV is not a germline-like class.** Among final UPVs with matched-normal WES pileup
   depth >=10, only 45/2,910 P4 alleles (1.55%) and 12/618 P6 alleles (1.94%) had an
   exact high-confidence WES match or the looser ALT-read evidence. The reciprocal
   98.5%/98.1% is an upper bound on a combined tumor-specific/RNA-specific/artifact
   remainder, not a somatic-rate estimate.
4. **The optional BAF/purity GMM is not stable.** It returned zero candidates in P4,
   P6, and DCIS2 under all 27 sensitivity settings. DCIS1 ranged from zero to 5,557;
   its production 5,557-site set is exactly the BAF<0.35 subset and disappears under
   nearby component/cutoff choices.
5. **Direct per-read allele-fraction contrasts do not rescue the GMM.** Median
   high-minus-low-purity delta-BAF was 0.000 (P4), 0.055 (P6, only 28 sites), 0.020
   (DCIS1), and 0.000 (DCIS2). The 1,744 evaluable DCIS1 GMM candidates divided into
   539 negative, 584 near-zero, and 621 positive values. Delta-BAF was essentially
   uncorrelated with pseudobulk BAF or minimum group depth.
6. **Purity association is strongly confounded and directionally inconsistent.** The
   production statistic is binary detection versus CalicoST purity, not BAF versus
   purity; it imputes missing purity as zero and floors negative correlations. After
   complete-case partial correlation controlling for log germline-detection burden,
   significant associations were all negative in P4, predominantly negative in P6,
   predominantly positive in DCIS1, and nearly balanced in DCIS2.
7. **Artifact evidence is substantial.** Exact editing/PON masks removed 24.6--40.8%
   of UPVs, and 19.5--49.3% were A>G/T>C. Same-build recurrence was artifact-heavy:
   214/382 shared P4--P6 alleles and 3,024/4,752 shared DCIS1--DCIS2 alleles were masked
   in both samples.

The recommended paper treatment is to keep UPV as a separately reported
spatial/prevalence-defined output, explicitly neutral about origin. Do not promote the
optional GMM subclass, do not merge UPVs into validated germline, and do not interpret
normal-WES absence as proof of somatic origin.

## How the analysis adapted to the data

The first analysis reproduced the existing pseudobulk BAF/purity GMM. It failed to
identify any candidate in three samples and labeled 5,557 DCIS1 sites. Because that
split could have been driven by the original feature definitions, the plan was expanded
in the following order:

1. Recompute BAF from I16 rather than the known-bad stored homozygous-ALT BAF field.
2. Compare UPV BAF against panel-defined germline within RNA-depth strata.
3. Sweep mixture components, seeds, and purity-centroid cutoffs.
4. Audit the production purity statistic, revealing zero imputation and negative-value
   flooring.
5. Recompute signed complete-case and burden-adjusted spot-level associations.
6. Test Stage-1 UPV membership itself over an alpha/beta grid.
7. Restrict recurrence to same-build comparisons.
8. Add exact editing/PON masks and substitution compatibility.
9. Scan CB-tagged BAMs directly for high-versus-low-purity delta-BAF.
10. Add matched-normal WES callability and ALT evidence when P1-1 completed.

At each step, a failed positive hypothesis was retained as a result. No threshold was
selected after seeing matched-normal truth, and no production class was rewritten.

## P1-1: matched-normal WES validation

### Design

- Truth: biallelic autosomal PASS/unfiltered SNVs in the matched-normal GATK VCF,
  normal-DNA DP>=10 and GQ>=20.
- Observation: targeted whole-Visium mpileup at every truth position, including misses;
  mapping quality >=20 and base quality >=13.
- Detection: exact chromosome, position, reference, and alternate allele in the union
  of final panel-defined and UPV VCFs.
- Strata: RNA depth, WES 0/1 versus 1/1, and exact 1KGP membership.
- Raw GT: `temp_gt_inferred.vcf`, because final class VCFs omit FORMAT.
- Model class: neural prediction VCF, only where a decision exists.
- Reverse check: targeted normal-WES pileup at every final germline/UPV allele.
- Intervals: Wilson 95% confidence intervals for proportions.

The completed detail tables contain 273,120 WES truth alleles and 178,879 final SPARCAL
germline/UPV alleles, with zero duplicate exact alleles in either table.

| sample | final class | WES-callable n | exact high-confidence n (%) | exact or ALT-evidence n (%) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| P4 | 1KGP-defined | 4,981 | 4,026 (80.83%) | 4,047 (81.25%) |

`WES-callable` here means usable matched-normal pileup DP>=10. Exact support requires the

these are support bounds rather than calibrated precision/specificity estimates.

### P1-1 conclusion and incomplete comparator

P1-1 supports a strong but narrow manuscript statement: **at expressed, adequately
covered matched-normal sites, the 1KGP-resolved SPARCAL germline set has approximately
80% sensitivity and normal-DNA support, with raw genotype agreement near 90%.** It does
not support general non-panel germline recovery, UPV-as-germline, or SparcalNet genotype
correction.

The updated work plan requested same-site GATK and Strelka2 RNA comparisons. No current
P4/P6 RNA-derived GATK or Strelka2 callset was found in the inspected project/data trees.
Tumor-WES calls and DLPFC callsets were not substituted. This comparator remains open
and requires new, harmonized RNA-caller runs on the same BAMs and exact WES truth sites.

## Deep UPV assessment

### Stage-1 membership is threshold-defined

The live Stage-1 gate uses spatial uniformity alpha and transformed global prevalence
beta. Output reproduction established production alpha/beta values of 0.5/0.4 for P4
and P6 and 0.5/0.2 for both DCIS sections. P4's 0.3/0.2 launch-script lines were comments
and were not used.

| sample | de novo candidates | production UPVs | grid minimum | grid maximum | minimum Jaccard with production |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| P4 | 204,725 | 9,491 | 4,299 | 20,961 | 0.325 |
| P6 | 658,296 | 1,744 | 1,289 | 5,630 | 0.310 |
| DCIS1 | 238,481 | 53,120 | 11,586 | 53,120 | 0.218 |
| DCIS2 | 287,863 | 36,323 | 9,146 | 36,323 | 0.252 |

The grid was fixed in advance at alpha 0.3/0.4/0.5/0.6 and beta
0.2/0.3/0.4/0.5. It is a stability analysis, not post hoc optimization.

### Pseudobulk BAF and defined-germline controls

All four UPV distributions have median BAF 1.0, as do the full panel-defined controls.
This reflects many low-depth homozygous-alternate RNA pileups and makes the global
median uninformative. Low-BAF fractions and Wasserstein distances were:

| sample | UPV n | UPV BAF<0.35 | defined BAF<0.35 | Wasserstein distance |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| P4 | 9,491 | 6.12% | 5.63% | 0.015 |
| P6 | 1,744 | 6.19% | 5.74% | 0.049 |
| DCIS1 | 53,120 | 10.46% | 5.37% | 0.117 |
| DCIS2 | 36,323 | 9.73% | 4.89% | 0.060 |

Low BAF was concentrated at intermediate RNA depth in both UPV and defined controls.
For example, at DP5--9 the low-BAF fractions were 34.6% versus 9.3% (P4), 21.7% versus
10.1% (P6), 24.4% versus 10.4% (DCIS1), and 30.2% versus 8.7% (DCIS2). BAF alone cannot
assign origin because depth, allelic expression, copy number, and mapping bias all act
on pseudobulk RNA allele fraction.

### Optional GMM instability

The production diagnostic fits three full-covariance components to BAF and the stored
purity correlation, then requires a low-BAF/positive-correlation component, posterior
>0.5, and per-site BAF<0.35. Across 2/3/4 components, three seeds, and purity-centroid
cutoffs 0.05/0.10/0.15:

| sample | production candidates | grid minimum | grid maximum |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| P4 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| P6 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| DCIS1 | 5,557 | 0 | 5,557 |
| DCIS2 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

All DCIS1 component centroids had similar positive stored purity-correlation means. The
selected component was distinguished primarily by low BAF, explaining why its 5,557
records exactly equal the BAF<0.35 subset and why the split disappears under nearby
settings.

### The production purity feature does not test BAF versus purity

`PURITY_CORR` is a point-biserial correlation of binary spot detection with CalicoST
purity. Production replaces missing CalicoST purity by zero and returns
`max(0, correlation)`. Missing-purity and detection-burden diagnostics were:

| sample | spot files | observed purity | imputed zero | complete-case purity vs log detection burden r |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| P4 | 744 | 427 | 42.6% | -0.321 |
| P6 | 3,650 | 2,369 | 35.1% | -0.239 |
| DCIS1 | 1,454 | 1,410 | 3.0% | +0.250 |
| DCIS2 | 1,807 | 1,320 | 27.0% | +0.071 |

After complete-case partial point-biserial correlation controlling for log per-spot
germline-detection burden:

| sample | q<0.05 total | positive | negative | positive with r>=0.10 | median adjusted r, all UPVs |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| P4 | 15 | 0 | 15 | 0 | +0.008 |
| P6 | 1,097 | 216 | 881 | 76 | -0.047 |
| DCIS1 | 35,770 | 33,642 | 2,128 | 14,722 | +0.072 |
| DCIS2 | 2,349 | 1,140 | 1,209 | 480 | +0.013 |

Large counts of small associations are expected with tens of thousands of variants and
hundreds/thousands of spots. Their sample-wide direction reversal is incompatible with
a common somatic-UPV mechanism. In DCIS1, the median clone-2-minus-clone-1 prevalence
was only +0.027 across all UPVs and +0.024 in the low-BAF/GMM subset.

| sample | exact editing/PON mask | A>G or T>C |

|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|

### Direct read-level delta-BAF

Low-pseudobulk-BAF UPVs were scanned in the CB-tagged BAM. Reference and alternate
reads were pooled across the lowest and highest CalicoST-purity tertiles at MQ>=20,
BQ>=13; both groups required at least five usable reads.

| sample | low-BAF scanned | evaluable | median delta-BAF | negative <-0.05 | near zero | positive >0.05 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| P4 | 585 | 222 | 0.0000 | 75 | 92 | 55 |
| P6 | 108 | 28 | +0.0548 | 6 | 8 | 14 |
| DCIS1 | 5,584 | 1,757 | +0.0196 | 544 | 587 | 626 |
| DCIS2 | 3,550 | 864 | 0.0000 | 195 | 425 | 244 |

Among the 1,744 evaluable DCIS1 GMM candidates specifically, the split was 539/584/621
and the mean was -0.0055. Spearman correlations of delta-BAF with pseudobulk BAF ranged
from -0.042 to +0.026; correlations with minimum group depth ranged from -0.038 to
-0.003. In DCIS1 and DCIS2, both positive and negative tails were more artifact/editing-
enriched than the near-zero group. None of the WES-callable P4/P6 low-BAF variants in
any delta direction had normal-DNA ALT evidence.

This contrast directly measures RNA allele-fraction magnitude, but it is exploratory:
reads within spots and spots within one tissue are not independent, and CalicoST purity
comes from the same RNA assay.

### UPV conclusion

The evidence excludes two simple interpretations:

- **Not validated germline:** matched-normal support among callable UPVs is below 2%.
- **Not a validated somatic class:** most sites lack normal-DNA evidence, but the same
  sites carry substantial editing/PON evidence; purity effects reverse across samples;
  the GMM is unstable; and direct delta-BAF is near zero or bidirectional.

UPV should remain an origin-neutral output. A future origin classifier requires an
independent truth set with matched normal and tumor DNA, not another threshold fitted to
the same spatial RNA/CalicoST features.

## Literature review and verified bibliography

The literature review changed the interpretation in four ways:

1. **RNA is not DNA truth.** RNA variant evidence is restricted by expression and depth
   and is altered by allele-specific expression, mapping bias, and RNA editing. This is
   why P1-1 conditions on RNA depth and why pseudobulk BAF is not called DNA VAF.
2. **Population-aware methods validate the panel-specific result.** 1KGP/Beagle and
   Monopogen provide the relevant population-linkage context, while Monovar,
   cellsnp-lite, scAllele, and SComatic frame sparse single-cell/RNA variant inference.
3. **Spatial methods use different information boundaries.** SpatialSNV, STMut,
   CalicoST, and the SpaceTracer preprint do not supply interchangeable truth sets;
   their inputs and use of CNV/purity must be described precisely.
4. **Purity/CNV and tumor catalogs are contextual evidence.** ASCAT, ABSOLUTE, FACETS,
   COSMIC/CGC, and mutational-signature work support interpretation, not automatic
   validation of an RNA-only allele.

The companion audit contains **57 bibliography entries, all 57 cited in the guided
manuscript, zero uncited entries, and zero missing citation keys**. Every DOI was checked
one by one against its registry and a primary publisher/PubMed/PMC/preprint record; the
two no-DOI resources were checked at their official project/vendor pages. SpaceTracer
and Mutect2 are explicitly labeled preprints. A scientifically disputed 2011 broad
RNA--DNA-difference claim was removed in favor of the stronger RNA-seq variant and
editing literature.
